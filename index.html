GPC Ecosystem Simulator — v0.30

Tiny number-crunching ecology lab. No ecosystem was harmed in the making of this prototype. Probably.

About simulation behavior

“Starting population” is initialization data and Reset restores it. Calibration uses Auto-balance as a seed, then pre-simulates the non-invasive ecosystem to estimate its healthy long-term equilibrium. Ecology now resolves monthly while authoring values stay annual. Producers regenerate edible biomass from habitat capacity rather than surviving producer count. Juveniles are intentionally not modeled yet. Consumer births are distributed across monthly ticks and enter the active population immediately after their birth month, so they can participate in feeding and predation on the following month. A yearly breeding-pool cap keeps those newborns from increasing reproductive eligibility until the following simulated year. Predation resolves before surviving prey feed. Producers use only starting population, carrying capacity, annual regrowth, edible biomass, and predation rating in the editor. Monthly diagnostics expose the math step by step. Food allocation now prioritizes survival before reproduction: basic survival rations are funded first, and only remaining surplus can make animals reproduction-eligible. Reproduction Surplus sets both the extra food cost and the maximum reproductive tendency. Species marked invasive are excluded from calibration. The run-controls checkbox can include or exclude all invasive species from simulation; turning it on explicitly introduces them at their authored Starting Population at the current year, while turning it off removes them from active simulation. Native Expected Population is estimated from the mature pre-invasion ecosystem, and native Starting Population is set to that baseline. Invasive Starting Population remains its authored introduction size. Expected Population is not a cap: populations may overshoot it freely. For animal prey below that healthy reference, encounterability declines in proportion to current/expected abundance, making scarce prey harder for predators to locate before they are eaten to extinction. Consumer populations below one individual are now treated as extinct and set to zero at the end of the monthly tick.

Calibrate healthy ecosystem

Pick one native consumer as the anchor. Auto-balance creates a native-only seed, then hidden pre-simulation estimates the mature healthy baseline. The mature native baseline is rescaled so the calibrated anchor matches the Anchor Population exactly; all native Starting and Expected populations scale by the same factor. Producer carrying capacities scale with them to preserve the calibrated ecology. Invasive species keep their authored introduction population.

Population history

Each species gets a categorical population timeline relative to Expected Population. Native species use the calibrated pre-invasion baseline; invasive species use their authored introduction population as a display reference. Extinct = 0; Scarce = below 50%; Stable = 50–150%; Abundant = 150–250%; Overpopulated = 250% or more. Labels below each bar mark every year in which the rating changes.

Visualization output

Turns simulation state into an illustration brief. Population uses Expected Population. Body condition uses food sufficiency. Babies, carcasses (natural + starvation deaths), and remains (predation deaths) are compared as per-capita annual rates against the native healthy calibration distribution. “Normal” is the middle 80% of healthy calibration years (10th–90th percentile), so the ecosystem defines its own normal variation. Invasive species have no pre-invasion healthy baseline, so rate-based outputs show —.
